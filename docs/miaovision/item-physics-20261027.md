## Diagnosis

All old vending drops used the same .22 half-extent cube, almost pure yaw, identical side outlets, and AABB-only unilateral vertical supports. Empty corners of rotated AABBs could support other items. This creates upright columns even when the visible sprite is thin. Some mod INVENTORY handlers draw flat GUI icons, despite the scene fallback already supporting extrusion. The room camera centered its 8-block simulation height rather than its roughly 4.4-block visual height. Door openness was displayed as raw 5 Hz samples.

## Independent implementation and contract

- Old `SceneSimulation(layout,bounds)` semantics remain unchanged. The new explicit `ContactModel.ORIENTED_BOX` mode uses a broad-phase AABB reject and at most 15 separating axes, bounded face/edge impact torque, and damped broad-face settlement. Three substeps, four sweeps, maximum 100 bodies; no per-pair heap allocation. This remains a display solver, not a general-purpose rigid-body engine or pixel-alpha collision solver.
- Construction, `put`, restoration, and rendering never enable simulation. Vending delivery/removal explicitly enables it; settled/disabled `step()` returns before body iteration/allocation. Saved trajectories and sleep counters are deterministic.
- Native blocks use .44 unit geometry proxies; ordinary sprites use .54 by .54 by .054 proxies. Quaternion rotation applies to both model and collision shape. `SceneSpriteTransform` maps the native 1/16 extrusion to exactly those physical half extents; no camera-facing billboard.
- Legacy room NBT without `modelShapeVersion` retains exact ID, center, rotation, velocities and sleep counters while reducing ordinary-item depth. No stock or output ownership changes, no replayed emission and no restore activation. A persisted machine-owned `migrationSettlePending` records only an actual legacy shape change. The next normal owner tick consumes that one-time event only if physics is allowed, explicitly wakes the pile, and lets it settle finitely. If physics is disabled, pending survives saves and poses remain unchanged until allowed; modern saves without the pending flag never auto-activate.
- Generic native sprites retain per-stack icon lookup, bounded multipass rendering, tint and enchantment glint. Custom-only/null-icon and block-helper models retain native fallback. Core's new `extrudeInventorySprites` resolver option defaults false.
- GTIT uses its already-installed GregTech renderer through **exact, pinned renderer-class contracts**, preserving audited charged/meta icon, material tint, overlay/fluid, tool and mechanical-armor paths. It does not assume every `IGT_ItemWithMaterialRenderer` is a planar sprite: tesseracts use centered native INVENTORY geometry with isotropic .8 normalization, while TranscendentMetal/UIV rotating effects use isotropic .65 normalization and common-side volume proxies. The renderer's `renderInFrame` ambient flag is saved, cleared and restored in `finally`. Unknown target classes retain native INVENTORY fallback rather than inheriting a thin EQUIPPED transform. It does not recreate or copy the native implementations. Primary contract: https://github.com/GTNewHorizons/GT5-Unofficial/blob/5.09.54.205/src/main/java/gregtech/common/render/items/MetaGeneratedItemRenderer.java and https://github.com/GTNewHorizons/GTNHLib/blob/master/src/main/java/com/gtnewhorizon/gtnhlib/util/ItemRenderUtil.java . EQUIPPED uses native extruded unit geometry without ENTITY billboard/bobbing or INVENTORY pixel transforms.
- Full-height walls remain real rendering/picking occluders. Their thickness lies outside simulation bounds. Floor/seams lie at or below physical floor zero; otherwise a flat .054-thick sprite would be swallowed by the old .062-high decorative floor. No items are hidden as a camera workaround.
- Door and body interpolation share the client presentation timeline. Newly feeding bodies interpolate from their real queued shell pose. Side travel starts inside the shell and only protrudes after the door clearance interval. Business delivery/ME clocks never depend on a client acknowledgement.

## Evidence and limits

Pure JVM contracts (no world/GUI/GL context): legacy contract suite; 100 mixed block/thin proxies; exact save/resume; floor-bounds validation; SAT non-intersection check; all 75 thin items lying flat; finite settled idle; 100 sequential drops from one outlet spread rather than form a tower. Render transform arithmetic is tested independently. Native GL pixels and interactive in-world appearance remain unverified: the user explicitly limits this delivery to main-menu startup/exit, without world, server, gameplay or GUI-interaction tests. Those checks are outside scope, not pending delivery gates; source and arithmetic checks are not a claim of GUI testing.

### Pinned GT dispatch audit follow-up

The public material interface omits the dispatcher's per-metadata special-renderer registry, and render-helper flags do not distinguish animated 3D planes from simple sprites. Therefore GTIT performs a read-only selection of the exact `gregtech.common.render.items.MetaGeneratedItemRenderer#getRendererForItemStack(ItemStack)` decision, caching only one Method after checking argument, return type, instance-method status and declaring class. If unavailable, it retains native INVENTORY handling. No reflective field writes, mixins, global patches, entities, or lookup retry loop are introduced. The supported implementation is the pinned GT5-Unofficial 5.09.54.205; unknown renderer subclasses are intentionally not admitted by inheritance.

Independent bytecode review identified true 3D `WireFrameTesseractRenderer`, rotating `TranscendentMetalRenderer`/`TranscendentalMetaItemRenderer`, and the non-IGT `MetaGeneratedToolRenderer`. The shared common-side proxy classifier uses initialized ItemList identities (Tesseract/EnergisedTesseract, ten UIV/T4Sawblade special items) and the TranscendentMetal material association, never loading a client renderer on the server. Pure `VendingNativeModelPolicyTest` verifies exact dispatch whitelist, unknown/inventory-only rejection, tool path and worst-case native vertex radii inside the chosen proxy. Unknown custom model geometry remains subject to its original Forge INVENTORY contract; this is not a claim of arbitrary-mod model normalization.

## Round 2 opt-in item-light rig (0.1.26 candidate)

This subsection records the 0.1.26 implementation. Compilation and full headless check passed; it does not claim a native-visual PASS. Earlier 0.1.25 evidence remains bound to its own source/artifact. Final compilation/headless and exact-pair main-menu startup receipts are recorded separately; world/server/gameplay/GUI interaction remains outside the authorized runtime scope.

`SceneModelResolver.dioramaItemLighting()` is a new **default-false** capability. Existing consumers retain their prior lighting; GTIT explicitly opts in. The brighter two-sided diorama rig is established before each body's rotation and nonuniform scale, rather than rotating/stretching the lights with thin item geometry. Constant light vectors use cached buffers, not per-item buffer allocation. Ambient/key/fill budget retains directional relief; this is not globally forcing all native materials to fullbright.

The rig belongs to the native-item pass, not room art or global world lighting. Native block/sprite, tint, glint and audited custom-renderer paths remain in use; custom emissive effects may retain their own material behavior. State changes must stay inside Core's existing complete GL-state/Tessellator restoration scope. Actual GL pixels and arbitrary-mod renderer compatibility are not established by arithmetic checks.

No contact-model default, old constructor, default-OFF lifecycle or WorkGovernor API is changed. Physics activation/migration remains consumer-owned. GTIT's 2.0.11 block-size policy (.78-unit native block-mesh edge; native sprite-only block items remain thin), 20-degree-left default view, real inventory capacity and ME scheduling are consumer policies, not new Core defaults. Read-only ItemPhysic provenance/no-copy boundaries above remain unchanged.
